## Player-facing objective

After `P4.2` the countryside is readable but hollow: a player follows a paved `7.0 m` primary from a city gate, crosses the Vltava-like bridge `+0.35` over teal water `4a7a94` `bank 9 m` `floodplain 26 m`, arrives at a hamlet clearing with hedgerow dressing and `2-6` low-rise shells (plaster `ddd0c0` brick `b07a5a` timber `7a5a3a` roof `8a3a2a`/`5a5a5a`) set back `4 m` from the grey/gravel ribbon — and finds empty boxes. The door obeys the `Door` contract, but there is nothing to shelter with, nothing to take, no reason to return. `P4.3` makes every rural shell a usable shelter: a deterministic interior partition (one wall dividing the box into `2` rooms where footprint allows), `1-3` furniture proxies (bed `1.9x0.9`, shelf `1.2x0.4`, table `1.0x1.0` or stove `0.8x0.8`) placed against walls, and a deterministic scavenge crate (`FoodCrate` with `1-4` items from `ItemDB` — `canned_food`, `water_bottle`, `bandage`, `antibiotics`) inside the larger room. The player can walk `600-900 m` along a rural corridor, spot a hamlet from a hill, open a door with `E`, step inside a `6x8`--`10x14` m shell, see a partition doorway, loot a crate with prompt `Search shelves (Canned Food x2)` via `E`, and take shelter — a tangible payoff that gives exploration a return, survival a countryside recovery alternative to the city, and the road graph a reason to be followed twice. Future milestones will expand these anchors into full building programs with workstations, habitation, economy and NPCs; this slice proves interior siting, streaming and loot honesty at the same budgeted discipline as shells.

This is the next bounded milestone because the geographic logic stack is landed and visually complete — terrain `17x17 289/512 1 collider 9 active`, hydrology `Vltava CX 530-710 meander 72+18 width 38-50 9x9 81/128 9 active`, biome/geology `9x9 81/128 <=48 instances 9 active`, settlement `12-36 spaced 700/420/220+1.8r +4-8 gates`, road `MST+1-3 loops` with ribbon `96/64 typical 160/96 junction ACTIVE-only 9 active`, rural shells `1-6 per settlement road-setback 4 spacing 8 192/144 typical 320/240 dense 1 collider 9 active doors <=6` — each proved deterministic, seam-continuous within `0.02` and ACTIVE-only. The hinterland has places, paths and walls but no interior function; without anchors every `rural_building_at(p)` shelter is empty, road-following has a weak arrival, and future farm production, workshop inhabitation and village economy would be placed over blank boxes. `P4.3` is the macro plan's "geographic logic precedes decoration" completion for Phase 4 habitability — interiors and scavenge must be established before full building programs (Phase 7), underground refuge, or upper-city retrofit can be populated.

This slice is deliberately thin and additive: no new parcel street grid inside villages, no village market square or fence enclosure, no full tenement/barn/farmhouse room graph (bedrooms/kitchen/stairs), no modular GLB assets, no rail mesh, no lake/reservoir basin formation, no terrain trench carve through the `350 m` historic basin, no underground caves, no toon shader rework, no NPC spawns inside rural shells. It adds deterministic interior siting as pure plan data plus one low-cost per-chunk interior augmentation streamed with the same ACTIVE-only budget discipline as shells.

## Gameplay value

Improves at least four enjoyment pillars in one thin slice while preserving budgets:

- **Survival pressure with recovery** — each village/hamlet now provides immediate recoverable resources: `1-3` crates per village (`1` per hamlet/farmstead, rarely `2` in a `6`-building village) with deterministic `canned_food`/`water_bottle`/`bandage`/`antibiotics` from `ItemDB`. Failure in the city (hunger/thirst/infection, zombie pressure) allows believable countryside recovery without inventing a new system; looting is deliberate (`E` per item, `FoodCrate` prompt updates, empty disables) and persists via deltas.
- **Exploration and discovery** — rural shells become readable shelters with a partition doorway visible through the exterior door, furniture silhouettes, and a crate landmark. A player can orient by "the hamlet shelf-house north of the ford" and return by the same road; interiors are visible from the doorway without entering, distinct across `256 m` landscape cells, not per-chunk speckles.
- **Agency and meaningful choice** — road vs cross-country now has a loot payoff gradient: villages (more buildings, more crates) reward longer `600-900 m` walks and bridge crossing, hamlets offer quick single-crate recovery with less exposure. The player chooses which settlement kind to target based on need and risk.
- **Coherent Czech identity** — Bohemian hamlet morphology plus interior use (bed against wall, shelf, table/stove) plus `canned_food`/`water_bottle` vernacular crate contents makes the basin recognizably inhabited without claiming a 1:1 map; furniture proxies use the same vertex-color palette discipline as shells.
- **Performance and stability foundation** — interiors reuse the measured streaming discipline: deterministic manifests, world-space building-center ownership, bounded verts/tris/colliders, ACTIVE-only physics, `t_rural_gen`/`t_rural_mat` telemetry within `FRAME_BUDGET_MS 12`, save exclusion of generated shells/interiors/crates (only `seed/version/discovery/deltas` plus crate deltas), `GENERATOR_VERSION` stays 2 (additive outside dense core).

Cosmetic polish accompanies but does not replace the playable system: interiors are thin vertex-colored boxes and small furniture proxies plus primitive crate `BoxMesh`, not imported modular assets or shader rework; the crate uses the existing `FoodCrate.gd` class and `ItemDB` table.
